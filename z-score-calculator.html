What Is a Z-Score?

A z-score (standard score) tells you how many standard deviations a value is from the mean. A positive z-score means the value is above the mean; a negative z-score means it's below.

Z-Score Formula

z = (X - μ) / σ

Where:

  • X = the raw value
  • μ = the mean of the population
  • σ = the standard deviation

The Empirical Rule and Z-Scores

For a normal distribution:

  • 68% of data has z-scores between -1 and +1
  • 95% of data has z-scores between -2 and +2
  • 99.7% of data has z-scores between -3 and +3

When to Use Z-Scores

Comparing Different Tests

Z-scores let you compare results from different tests with different scales. For example, comparing an SAT score to an ACT score by converting both to z-scores.

Finding Percentiles

Z-scores tell you what percentage of values fall below a given point. Useful for understanding where a data point ranks in a distribution.

Detecting Outliers

Values with |z| > 3 are considered outliers in a normal distribution. Z-scores help identify unusual data points in quality control and research.

Hypothesis Testing

Z-scores are fundamental to z-tests and confidence intervals. They form the basis of many statistical inference procedures.

Example

A student scores 85 on a test where the mean is 70 and the standard deviation is 10.

  1. z = (85 - 70) / 10 = 15 / 10 = 1.5
  2. This means the score is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean
  3. The percentile is approximately the 93.3rd percentile — the student scored higher than about 93.3% of test-takers
