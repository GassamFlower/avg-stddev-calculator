What Is Variance?

Variance measures how far a set of numbers is spread out from their mean. It is the average of the squared differences from the mean. A higher variance indicates that the data points are more spread out.

Variance Formulas

Sample Variance

Used when your data is a subset of a larger population:

s² = Σ(xᵢ - x̄)² / (n - 1)

Population Variance

Used when your data represents the entire population:

σ² = Σ(xᵢ - μ)² / N

Variance vs. Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance. While variance is measured in squared units (e.g., meters²), standard deviation is in the original units (e.g., meters), making it easier to interpret. Both measure the same thing — spread of data — but standard deviation is more commonly reported.

Variance ValueWhat It Means
s² = 0All values are identical (no spread at all)
s² is smallData points are close to the mean
s² is largeData points are widely spread

How to Calculate Variance by Hand

  1. Find the mean of the data set
  2. Subtract the mean from each value
  3. Square each difference
  4. Add all the squared differences (this is the "sum of squares")
  5. Divide by (n − 1) for sample variance, or by n for population variance

Example

Data set: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10

  1. Mean = (2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10) / 5 = 30 / 5 = 6
  2. Differences: (2−6) = −4, (4−6) = −2, (6−6) = 0, (8−6) = 2, (10−6) = 4
  3. Squared: 16, 4, 0, 4, 16
  4. Sum of squares = 16 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 40
  5. Sample variance = 40 / (5−1) = 40 / 4 = 10
  6. Population variance = 40 / 5 = 8
