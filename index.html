How to Calculate Average and Standard Deviation

Average (Mean)

The average is the sum of all values divided by the number of values. It represents the central tendency of your data:

x̄ = (Σxᵢ) / n

Standard Deviation

Standard deviation measures how spread out the data is from the mean. It is one of the most widely used measures of variability in statistics.

Sample Standard Deviation

Use this when your data is a sample from a larger population. The division by (n − 1) is called Bessel's correction:

s = √[ Σ(xᵢ - x̄)² / (n - 1) ]

Population Standard Deviation

Use this when your data represents the entire population:

σ = √[ Σ(xᵢ - μ)² / N ]

When to Use Each

  • Sample standard deviation (s): Use when analyzing a subset of data to estimate the variability of a larger population. This is the most common case in research, surveys, and quality control.
  • Population standard deviation (σ): Use when you have data for every member of the population you're studying, such as test scores for an entire class or measurements of every item in a batch.

Understanding Your Results

The standard deviation tells you how much variation exists in your data. Here's how to interpret it:

Standard Deviation What It Means Example
Low (close to 0) Data points are clustered tightly around the mean Test scores: 78, 80, 82, 79, 81 (s ≈ 1.6)
Moderate Data has a reasonable spread around the mean Test scores: 65, 72, 80, 88, 95 (s ≈ 11.4)
High Data points are spread over a wide range Test scores: 30, 50, 70, 90, 100 (s ≈ 27.4)

The Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7)

For data that follows a normal (bell-shaped) distribution:

  • 68% of data falls within 1 standard deviation of the mean
  • 95% of data falls within 2 standard deviations of the mean
  • 99.7% of data falls within 3 standard deviations of the mean

This rule is useful for identifying outliers — data points beyond 3 standard deviations from the mean are very unusual in a normal distribution.

Real-World Use Cases

Finance & Investing

Standard deviation measures investment volatility. A stock with high standard deviation has unpredictable returns, while low standard deviation indicates stable performance. Used in portfolio risk analysis and Value at Risk (VaR) calculations.

Education & Testing

Teachers use standard deviation to understand score distribution. A low SD means most students scored similarly; a high SD suggests a wide gap between high and low performers. Helps identify whether a test was appropriately difficult.

Quality Control

Manufacturers use standard deviation to monitor product consistency. Six Sigma methodology aims for processes within 6 standard deviations. Low SD in product measurements means consistent quality.

Science & Research

Researchers report standard deviation alongside means to show data reliability. It's essential for hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and determining whether results are statistically significant.

Example: Calculating Standard Deviation Step by Step

Let's calculate the standard deviation for this data set: 10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21, 15

  1. Find the mean: (10 + 12 + 23 + 23 + 16 + 23 + 21 + 15) / 8 = 143 / 8 = 17.875
  2. Find squared differences: (10−17.875)² = 62.02, (12−17.875)² = 34.52, ... for each value
  3. Sum of squared differences: 62.02 + 34.52 + 26.02 + 26.02 + 3.52 + 26.02 + 9.77 + 8.27 = 224.875
  4. Sample variance: 224.875 / (8−1) = 32.125
  5. Sample standard deviation: √32.125 ≈ 5.67

Frequently Asked Questions

What is standard deviation?

Standard deviation is a measure of how spread out numbers are from the mean (average). A low standard deviation means the data points are close to the average, while a high standard deviation means they are spread over a wider range. It is calculated as the square root of the variance.

When should I use sample vs. population standard deviation?

Use sample standard deviation (s) when your data is a subset of a larger population — this is the most common case in research, surveys, and quality control. Use population standard deviation (σ) only when you have data for every member of the population, such as test scores for an entire class.

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance is the average of the squared differences from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of variance. Standard deviation is more commonly used because it is expressed in the same units as the original data, making it easier to interpret. For example, if your data is in meters, the standard deviation is also in meters, while the variance is in square meters.

How do you calculate standard deviation by hand?

Follow these 5 steps: (1) Find the mean of the data. (2) Subtract the mean from each value and square the result. (3) Add all the squared differences together. (4) Divide by n−1 (for sample) or n (for population) to get the variance. (5) Take the square root of the variance to get the standard deviation.

What does a high standard deviation mean?

A high standard deviation means the data values are spread out over a wide range from the mean. In practical terms, this indicates high variability or inconsistency. For example, if two classes have the same average test score but different standard deviations, the class with the higher standard deviation has a wider gap between high and low performers.

What is the empirical rule (68-95-99.7 rule)?

For a normal (bell-shaped) distribution: approximately 68% of data falls within 1 standard deviation of the mean, 95% within 2 standard deviations, and 99.7% within 3 standard deviations. This rule helps you understand how data is distributed and identify potential outliers — values beyond 3 standard deviations are very unusual.
