What Is a Confidence Interval?

A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter (such as the mean). A 95% confidence interval means that if you were to take many samples and compute a confidence interval from each, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true population mean.

Confidence Interval Formula

CI = x̄ ± z* × (s / √n)

Where:

  • x̄ = sample mean
  • z* = critical value (based on confidence level)
  • s = sample standard deviation
  • n = sample size
  • s / √n = standard error of the mean (SE)

Margin of Error

The margin of error is the half-width of the confidence interval. It tells you how much the sample estimate might differ from the true population value:

E = z* × (s / √n)

The margin of error decreases when:

  • Sample size (n) increases — more data means more precision
  • Standard deviation (s) decreases — less variability means more precision
  • Confidence level decreases — less certainty means a narrower interval

How to Interpret a Confidence Interval

If a poll reports "52% support, with a margin of error of ±3% at 95% confidence," the confidence interval is (49%, 55%). This means we are 95% confident that the true population support is between 49% and 55%.

Important: It is not correct to say "there is a 95% probability that the true mean is in this interval." The true mean is fixed; the interval is random. The 95% refers to the long-run frequency of intervals that would contain the true mean.

Example

A sample of n = 50 students has a mean test score of x̄ = 75 with standard deviation s = 10. Find the 95% confidence interval.

  1. Standard Error: SE = 10 / √50 = 10 / 7.071 = 1.414
  2. Critical value for 95%: z* = 1.960
  3. Margin of Error: E = 1.960 × 1.414 = 2.772
  4. CI = 75 ± 2.772 = (72.228, 77.772)

We are 95% confident that the true population mean test score is between 72.228 and 77.772.
