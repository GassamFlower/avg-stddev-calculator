What Is the Median?

The median is the middle value in a sorted data set. It divides the data into two equal halves: half the values are below the median, and half are above. Unlike the mean, the median is not affected by extreme values (outliers), making it a robust measure of central tendency.

How to Find the Median

Odd Number of Values

Sort the data and pick the middle value:

Median = value at position (n + 1) / 2

Example: For 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 (n=5), the median is the 3rd value = 5

Even Number of Values

Sort the data and average the two middle values:

Median = (value at position n/2 + value at position n/2 + 1) / 2

Example: For 1, 3, 5, 7 (n=4), the median = (3 + 5) / 2 = 4

When data is skewed (has extreme values), the median is usually a better representation of "typical" than the mean. For example, median income is more meaningful than mean income because a few very high earners can dramatically inflate the average.

Median, Mode, and Range

  • Mode: The value that appears most frequently. A dataset can have no mode, one mode, or multiple modes.
  • Range: The difference between the maximum and minimum values (Max − Min).
  • Together: Median, mode, and range are often reported alongside the mean to give a complete picture of a dataset's central tendency and spread.

Real-World Applications

Real Estate

Median home prices are reported instead of averages because a few luxury homes can skew the mean upward, misrepresenting the typical home price.

Income & Economics

Median income is the standard measure because income distributions are heavily right-skewed. The median represents what a "typical" person earns.

Test Scores

Teachers use the median to understand the "middle" student performance, especially when a few very high or low scores distort the average.

Healthcare

Median survival time is reported in medical studies because a few long-term survivors can inflate the mean, giving a misleading picture.
